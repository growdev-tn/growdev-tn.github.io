## 2. Rules of the house

These follow `docs/CREATIVE-BRIEF.md` §3 ("claims to avoid") and the book's own advice that a guarantee must be specific and real and that urgency must be genuine.

1. **No invented proof.** No fake clients, counters, ratings, testimonials or "as seen in". A client is shown only with their agreement, and nothing is said about the work until they approve the wording.
2. **No invented offers.** No "free", "from X DT" (no price at all: it depends on the client and on the complexity of the project), "guaranteed savings", deadline or response time on the public site until Aziz has decided it and can keep it.
3. **No false urgency or scarcity.** No countdowns, "only 3 spots left" or pop-ups. A real limit (for example, a real cap on new projects per quarter) may be stated once it is true.
4. **No credential that is not held.** No "official Odoo partner", "certified" or "#1" (open question 7 of the brief).
5. **No trackers and no cookies.** This is part of the offer ("No cookies, no trackers"). Measure with the e-mails themselves (§6). Anything that collects personal data (newsletter, form, analytics) needs Tunisian data-protection advice first (organic law 2004-63).
6. **Plain words.** A visitor with no IT background must understand every headline; technical detail lives in the developer section.
7. **Keep every public promise.** The page now says "A clear quote before we start" and "If it suits you, you receive a clear quote, split into phases". Those are commitments; if they stop being true, change the page the same day.

## 3. The nine squares

### Before: turning strangers into leads

**3.1 Target market**

*Today.* The brief names three markets: (1) Tunisian SMEs of 5–50 people in trading and distribution, retail, light manufacturing, services and clinics; (2) the Maghreb and French-speaking Africa; (3) start-ups and agencies abroad that want Odoo, web or mobile work from a Tunisian team. The pain is written down: files that live in Excel and WhatsApp, double entry, late invoices, no online presence.

*Gap.* "SMEs" is a category, not a person. A niche makes every other square cheaper: the message gets sharper and the channels become obvious.

*Proposal (to confirm).* Start from the client you wish you had a hundred of. Of the five on the site, which was the easiest to work with, the most satisfied and the most likely to refer? What do they have in common (sector, size, the situation that made them call)? That pattern is the niche. A draft to react to: *"Owners and managers of Tunisian trading and retail businesses of 5–50 people whose sales, stock and invoices live in Excel and WhatsApp, and who have just hit a trigger: a lost invoice, a stock-out, a second location, a decision to sell online."* The triggers are hypotheses to check with the five clients, not facts.

*A second, separate market.* Recruiters, agencies and technical teams who need a developer for an assignment. They want different things (stack, availability, how Aziz works), so the site gives them their own door: the hero link "Hiring a developer for a project?", the `#developpeur` section and the second contact door. Keep the two messages apart.

*Who it is not for* (proposal): someone who wants software without any involvement on their side, since the method needs a person on the client side who can answer questions; and one-off jobs below what is worth quoting. Aziz decides where that line is.

**3.2 Message (USP)**

*Today.* What is already true and visible: you talk to the person who writes the code; goals, schedule and deliverables are agreed before the work starts; code is clean, documented and backed up; there is support after launch; French, Arabic and English with real right-to-left Arabic; no cookies; this site's source code is public.

*Draft USP (FR, to confirm):* « Un seul interlocuteur, celui qui écrit le code : on vous explique sans jargon, on vous donne un devis clair avant de commencer, et on livre par étapes. »
*English:* "One contact, the person who writes the code: explained without jargon, a clear quote before we start, delivered in steps."

*Test.* Could a competitor copy this sentence onto their site? A larger agency cannot honestly promise "the person who writes the code", and a marketplace freelancer cannot promise a local, trilingual company. The sentence is specific enough to be wrong for some visitors, which is the point.

*Gap.* The USP has a mechanism but no guarantee behind it yet (square 6).

**3.3 Media**

Choose one or two channels that reach the niche, and give each a way to count. Ranked:

1. **The site, found through search and AI assistants.** Built in: three language versions with hreflang, structured data, `llms.txt`, a FAQ written as questions people ask. Cost: time. Count: Search Console impressions per language, plus the "How did you hear about GrowDev?" line (§6).
2. **Word of mouth through the five clients, and WhatsApp.** Tunisian owners ask friends and Facebook groups first (brief §2). Be present and useful where they ask, link only when it answers the question, never spam. Count: the same "how did you hear" line.
3. **LinkedIn and GitHub for the developer door.** GitHub (profile and repository) and Aziz's LinkedIn profile are linked from the technical profile and from the structured data (set on 24 Nov). A LinkedIn *company* page for GrowDev does not exist yet.

*Not now:* paid advertising. Until the niche, the guarantee and a lead magnet exist, paid traffic lands on a page that can only sell to people ready to buy today. Revisit after squares 1, 2, 4 and 6 are settled.

### During: turning leads into customers

**3.4 Lead capture**

*Today (ready-to-talk visitors).* The "Describe my project by email" link opens a mail with three short prompts (what takes your time, your business, how you heard about GrowDev). The project builder prepares a fuller brief. The simulator ends with "Talk about this result". The developer door opens an assignment template. All of them capture people who are already convinced.

*Gap.* Most first-time visitors are not ready. For them the only option is to leave. The book's advice is a specific, low-friction offer that solves one narrow problem and leads toward the paid work. "Free consultation" with no content is not an offer.

*Proposal (to confirm).* A one-page checklist, **"The 7 tasks to automate first in a small business"**, in French, Arabic and English. It teaches the visitor to spot their own time-wasters (the same skill the simulator measures) and ends by pointing at the situation guide and the simulator. Delivery without any form, tracker or third-party tool: a link that opens a mail ("Send me the checklist") or a WhatsApp message; Aziz replies with the file. The sender's address is the lead. Adding them to a newsletter is a separate step that needs their explicit yes.

*WhatsApp.* The button is on since 24 Nov (`"whatsapp"` in `src/site.json`) in the "You have a project" door, in all three languages, with a ready-made first message. The brief describes the pain as "Excel and WhatsApp", so owners already run their business there: it is the capture path to watch first, and the natural place to deliver the checklist.

**3.5 Lead nurture**

*Today.* Nothing is written. The site pre-answers the three objections almost every lead has (cost: FAQ 3; time: FAQ 4; "I won't understand it": FAQ 9), which makes the first conversation warmer, but there is no follow-up routine.

*Proposal: a permission-based sequence after a first contact (by mail or WhatsApp, in the visitor's language).*

| When | What | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Reply with two or three clear questions and what happens next, in the same words as the page | Keeps the promise made on the page |
| Day 2–3 | If no answer: one short, useful line (the checklist, or the FAQ answer that matches their situation) | Value, not pressure |
| Day 7 | A rough split into phases for what they described | Shows what working together looks like |
| Day 21 | A friendly check-in that also says it is fine if the timing is wrong | Respect; keeps the door open |
| Later | If they said "next quarter", note the date and write then | Follow-up that does not depend on memory |

Keep a plain sheet (§6). A CRM is not needed at this volume.

**3.6 Sales conversion**

*Today.* The contact section sets out the process: you write, we come back with clear questions and a first direction, and if it suits you, you receive a clear quote split into phases. A visitor always knows the next step. Five client logos give recognition; there is no case study and no guarantee.

*The real objection is risk, not price.* The book's IT-services example builds the guarantee around what buyers fear. Mapped to GrowDev:

| Buyer's fear | What the site does today | Decision still open |
|---|---|---|
| "I won't understand the jargon" | Plain words everywhere; FAQ 9; a guard in the checker that fails if jargon creeps into the hero, the situation guide or the contact text | none |
| "I will pay while you learn" | Quote split into phases | A concrete rule, such as phase 1 as a small slice judged before committing to phase 2 (only if quotes are really built that way) |
| "You will disappear / miss meetings" | One contact, a written scope | A commitment about response and meeting times, only if it can be kept |
| "The problems will come back" | "Support after launch: fixes and improvements" | What exactly is covered, and for how long |

*Candidate guarantees (pick one, only if true):* (a) a first phase small enough to judge before committing to the next; (b) you get the source, the documentation and a backup, so you are never locked in (needs contract wording); (c) if we cannot help, we say so frankly (FAQ 10 already says this to technical teams).

*Proof.* Ask each of the five clients for one sentence and a written yes. Prepare one case study in the client's own numbers (problem → what was built → result), with the client's approval of the wording. The brief suggests Aziz's own products (Werchti, IqrarSanté) as the first candidates, since no one else's permission is needed.

*Urgency.* None on the site, on purpose. A real limit may be stated once it exists.

### After: turning customers into fans

**3.7 World-class experience**

*Today.* The pledges describe the experience; nothing makes it routine. Proposals, all cheap:

1. **A welcome message in the first 48 hours** after "yes": who the single contact is, the written scope (goals, schedule, deliverables), what is needed from the client (files, logo, access) and what they will see by when.
2. **Two planned check-ins** after go-live (two weeks, three months) before anything breaks, matching the "support after launch" pledge.
3. **One unexpected touch**, chosen by Aziz: for example a one-page "how to use your new tool" in the client's language, or a note confirming that the backup was restored successfully as a test.

**3.8 Lifetime value**

The three levers, applied:

- **Price.** Quote on value and scope; do not discount to win. The site prints no prices on purpose, and since 25 Nov 2026 no figure at all (the simulator no longer turns hours into money): the price depends on the client and on the complexity of the project. FAQ 3 and the "Pricing and quotes" fact say what it depends on and that a clear quote follows a first conversation.
- **Transaction size.** The seven services already form a ladder: Odoo core → extra modules → a website or shop connected to Odoo → a mobile app → automation. Call each of the five clients about the next logical need.
- **Frequency.** Odoo hosting, backups and updates are already part of the offer (service card). Turning that into a monthly support plan is a decision: what it covers, what it costs, minimum term.

**3.9 Referrals**

*Today.* Never asked. Ask at the moment of peak satisfaction (right after a successful go-live), not at signing. Make it easy: a message the client can forward as it stands.

*Draft ask (FR):* « Ravi que le lancement se soit bien passé. Si vous connaissez une entreprise qui perd du temps comme vous en perdiez, pourriez-vous nous présenter ? Il suffit de transférer ce message : "Je travaille avec GrowDev, ils expliquent sans jargon et donnent un devis clair avant de commencer. Contact : contact@growdev.tn". »
*English:* "Glad the launch went well. If you know a business that loses time the way you used to, could you introduce us? Just forward this: 'I work with GrowDev. They explain things without jargon and give a clear quote before starting. contact@growdev.tn'."

*Incentive (decision 6):* a thank-you gift, a credit on the next phase, or none. A sincere thank-you and a return introduction are enough for many B2B relationships. An Arabic version needs a native read before use.

## 6. Measuring without trackers

The site collects nothing, so the e-mails are the data. Every first message now arrives with a recognisable subject line ("Projet GrowDev", "Brief projet GrowDev", "Mission de développement GrowDev" and their Arabic and English equivalents) and a line asking how the sender heard about GrowDev.

Keep one sheet, one row per enquiry:

| Date | Language | Door (project / assignment) | How they heard | First reply sent | Quote sent | Result (won / lost / later) | Value | Repeat or referral |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Read it monthly with Search Console:

| Metric | Source | Target from the brief |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified enquiries per month | count of rows | 3+ within 90 days of launch |
| Enquiry → quote, quote → project | sheet | set after the first ten rows |
| Average project value, share of repeat work | sheet | set after the first five projects |
| Referrals per client | sheet | at least one ask per project |
| Search impressions per language | Search Console | growing every month, all three languages present |

If a channel brings nothing after a quarter, drop it and put the time into the one that does. Depth beats breadth.

## 7. Psychology used, and where

Applied to remove friction, not to push (the long-term test: will the visitor be glad in six months?).

| Principle | Where |
|---|---|
| Jobs to be done | `#situations`: the visitor picks a job in their own words, not a service name |
| Curse of knowledge | A jargon guard in the checker; FAQ 9; technical language kept in `#developpeur` |
| Hick's law | Two buttons in the hero, six situations, two contact doors, three steps |
| Reciprocity | The simulator and the situation guide give value before asking for anything; the lead magnet continues this |
| Commitment and consistency | Small first step: tick a need, then a brief, then a mail with three short prompts |
| Loss framing | The simulator shows the hours the visitor loses today (not money, since 25 Nov 2026: the price of a project depends on the client and on its complexity), from their own numbers, with the formula written out so they can check it |
| Social proof and authority | Client logos with no invented detail; the public source code of this site |
| Peak-end | The page ends with "What happens next?", three steps |
| **Not used** | False scarcity or urgency, fake counters or reviews, pre-ticked consents, invented "was / now" prices |
